title: Should I switch back from Colemak to QWERTY?
date: 2025-11-22
categories: [keyboard]
description: After almost 10 years on Colemak, I'm debating switch back to QWERTY
---

I switched to Colemak almost 10 years ago. I had been interested in other keyboard layouts since first learning about Dvorak years earlier. I gave Dvorak a try, but the change was too drastic for me. When I learned about Colemak, I really liked the idea of a more ergonomic layout that still kept most of the standard shortcut keys in the same places as QWERTY. That eased the transition because I could rely on years of muscle memory for those shortcuts.

The transition to Colemak was difficult for me. I practiced typing with it daily for about a week and then tried using it for some tasks. I still switched back to QWERTY often when I needed to get something done quickly. Once I got up to about 15 WPM on Colemak, it basically broke me. I lost my ability to type on QWERTY. With my muscle memory for the two layouts mixed up, I couldn't type well on Colemak either. At that point, I had to decide whether to give up and relearn QWERTY or commit to Colemak. I stuck with Colemak and practiced when I could. It took me three difficult months to get back to a reasonable speed.

It took me more than a year of using Colemak full-time before I could do more than hunt and peck very slowly on a QWERTY keyboard.

Typing on Colemak feels more comfortable to me than typing on QWERTY did. It's hard to tell how much of that comes from the layout and how much comes from becoming a better typist over the years. I don't think my speed has improved much. I didn't take a formal typing test before switching, so I can't say how much it changed. I'm not a fast typer, but I am fast enough to be efficient. Depending on the test, I can reach 60–65 WPM on Colemak. My best guess is that I typed at about 55 WPM on QWERTY.

I used to have occasional wrist pain, and I switched from a mouse to a vertical trackball at the same time I switched layouts. I no longer have wrist pain, which I think has more to do with the trackball.

Why do I want to switch back after almost 10 years? For me, it comes down to a few small annoyances. My fingers feel a little more comfortable on Colemak, but I don't think that benefit outweighs the annoyances anymore.

* On keyboards like the one on my laptop, the key legends don't match the layout I use. I touch-type, so this rarely matters. When I need to press a single key, like at a `y/n` prompt in the terminal, I have to put my fingers on the home row and let muscle memory find the key.
* It is harder to get keyboards with Colemak key legends. I got a custom one soon after I switched and I've been using the same one since then.
* It's harder to set up Colemak on a phone. Support has improved since I first switched, though it's still uneven. On the iPhone, I need Gboard to use Colemak. Plus, my wife doesn't like using my phone with that layout.
* Vim keybindings are becoming more common in applications.
    * This is probably my biggest annoyance. I've tried to learn Vim keybindings, but only the basics have stuck.
    * In Helix, I've remapped the standard `hjkl` keys to `hnei`. That works okay, but I often forget to use those keys because I usually work in Zed with Vim keybindings turned off.
    * More applications support `hjkl`, and most of them don't let me remap those keys. It's hard to justify learning `hnei` when I can't use it everywhere.
    * I've gotten somewhat used to reaching for `j` and `k` on Colemak in the apps where I use them often. They're still easier to reach on QWERTY.
* I rarely need to use a QWERTY keyboard, but typing at about 20 WPM feels awkward when I do.

My main worry is that switching back to QWERTY could take a long time. Since I can already type at about 20 WPM, I think I could get back up to speed with a bunch of practice if I dropped Colemak entirely.

I also wonder whether I'd miss how comfortable Colemak feels. Plenty of people type comfortably on QWERTY, so I suspect the difference would be minor. Using just QWERTY might make things easier if I only have one set of key positions to remember. I'm not sure how long I'd keep reaching for the Colemak keys out of habit.
