It's been just over a year since I wrote about [whether I should switch back from Colemak to QWERTY](/blog/back-to-qwerty-from-colemak/). I ended up switching back soon after writing that post.

I was worried it would take a long time to get back up to speed, luckily it ended up not being the case. It took about a week for QWERTY to feel comfortable again and about a month to get my speed back to my Colemak levels. I'm up to about 75 WPM now, so even faster than I last tested on Colemak. I'm not sure why that is, maybe because I only have one layout in my brain now. I think I've completly lost the Colemak muscle memory at this point. Surprisingly, some of my old QWERTY bad habits came back such as using the wrong fingers for some keys, but it works for me. Even after all this time, it is like I never switched in the first place.

I do notice the comfort issues with QWERTY. Typing is a bit more awkward than and I notice the extra finger travel if I think about it, but in the long run it doesn't seem to matter.

It's nice to use my laptop keyboard with being able to see the keys again again. I can also buy a from a larger selection of keyboards since I don't need to find one that I have to modifiy. On my phone, I can use whichever keyboard I want without being limited to only ones that support Colemak, plus my wife can use my phone normally when she needs to.

Overall, I'm glad I switched back. The benefits of Colemak did not outweigh the costs for me and the reduced comfort of QWERTY is not a real issue worth spending time on anymore.
